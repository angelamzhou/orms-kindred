\section{Overview and privacy model}
ORMatch takes a manuscript PDF and returns a ranked list of candidate reviewers drawn from the authors of recent OR/MS papers, each with the papers that justify the match and any potential conflict of interest flagged with its evidence. The design separates a \emph{public index} (titles, abstracts, authorships, references and precomputed embeddings of published papers, built offline and distributed as a versioned archive) from a \emph{private query}: text extraction, embedding, search and ranking all run on the user's machine, and no text, embedding or metadata about the manuscript leaves it. The command \texttt{ormatch verify-offline} runs the full pipeline with Python's socket layer disabled to demonstrate this. The only network calls the tool ever makes are the one-time download of the index and of the embedding model weights.

\section{Data and provenance}
\paragraph{Source.} All bibliographic data come from OpenAlex (\url{https://openalex.org}, CC0), pulled through its REST API with cursor pagination (no result cap) for works with \texttt{publication\_year}$\ge$2014 whose primary location is one of 29 venues: the INFORMS journals (Operations Research, Management Science, M\&SOM, MOR, Transportation Science, IJOC, Stochastic Systems), Mathematical Programming, SIAM J.\ Optimization, SIAM J.\ Control and Optimization, POM, JOM, Decision Sciences, NRL, IISE/IIE Transactions, EJOR, ORL, Computers \& OR, Annals of OR, Omega, IJPE, IJPR, Computers \& IE, Transportation Research B and E, JOTA, JORS and Networks. Works that list a venue only as a non-primary location are included when they carry a DOI (this drops RePEc and repository records misattributed to the journals). Each pull is resumable and its state (cursor, counts, filter) is stored alongside the raw JSONL; \texttt{scripts/check\_pull.py} compares saved counts with live counts to detect drift.

\paragraph{Fields kept.} Title, abstract (reconstructed from OpenAlex's inverted index), year, venue, DOI, open-access URL, citation count, the full authorship list with author IDs and first-listed institution, and the outgoing reference list (\texttt{referenced\_works}).

\paragraph{Abstract backfill.} OpenAlex carries no abstracts for most Elsevier titles (EJOR 17\%, ORL 11\% before backfill). Missing abstracts are requested from the Semantic Scholar Graph API in batches of 500 DOIs; Crossref recovers essentially nothing for Elsevier and is optional. An optional pass against the Elsevier Article Retrieval API (institutional key) is implemented but has not been run.

\paragraph{Side tables.} Per-author statistics (works count, citations, $h$-index) come from the OpenAlex authors endpoint. Editorial-board membership is parsed from yearly Internet Archive snapshots (2016--2026) of 14 journals' board pages, since the publishers' sites refuse scripted access; each (journal, role, person) row records the first and last year seen. Advisor/student links come from the Mathematics Genealogy Project on request. Department rosters (167 OR/IE/OM departments in 25 countries) are scraped where sites permit.

\paragraph{Add-on collections.} Only about 29\% of the references in core papers point back into the core venues, so additional collections (statistics/ML, economics/finance, algorithms) can be built as self-contained index directories and searched together with the core. Authors who appear only in an add-on must have at least one link to the core literature (a core paper, or a citation to or from one) to be proposed, so the pool widens to people connected to OR rather than to arbitrary outsiders.

\section{Embeddings}
Each paper is represented by the SPECTER2 encoder (\texttt{allenai/specter2\_base} with the \texttt{proximity} adapter), a SciBERT-based model trained with citation-based contrastive objectives on titles and abstracts. The input is \texttt{title [SEP] abstract} (title only when the abstract is missing), truncated to 512 tokens; the embedding is the final-layer \texttt{[CLS]} vector, $\ell_2$-normalised, stored in float16 (768 dimensions, 47~MB for 31k papers, about 110~MB at the current size). SciNCL (\texttt{malteos/scincl}) is a drop-in alternative and TF-IDF+SVD a dependency-free fallback; the index records which backend built it and queries always use the same one. On an RTX~4090 the corpus embeds in about 200~s.

The manuscript is processed identically: \texttt{pypdfium2} extracts the first pages, heuristics recover title and abstract (the title is the leading lines before ``Abstract'' that do not look like an author line; the abstract runs to ``Introduction''/``Keywords''), and the same encoder produces the query vector $q$. Separately, the bibliography is parsed from the trailing pages (entries split on author-line starts, DOIs by regex, otherwise the sentence after the year as a title guess) and matched to indexed papers by DOI or normalised title.

\section{Matching}
Let $s_i=\langle q, e_i\rangle$ be the cosine between the manuscript and indexed paper $i$, and $P_a$ the papers of author $a$. The score is a linear combination of interpretable terms, all in cosine units:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{score}(a) \;=\;& (1-\lambda)\max_{i\in P_a}s_i \;+\; \lambda\,\mathrm{mean}_{\text{top-}k}(s_i)
 \;+\; w_{\mathrm{cite}}\bigl(1-0.5^{\,n_{\mathrm{cited}}(a)}\bigr)
 \;+\; w_{\mathrm{seed}}\bigl(\sigma_a-\mathrm{med}\,\sigma\bigr)
 \;+\; w_{\mathrm{early}}\,\phi(\mathrm{works}_a)\\
 &-\; w_{\mathrm{vol}}\bigl(\mathbb E[\max\text{ of }|P_a|\text{ random }s]-\mu_s\bigr)
 \;-\; w_{\mathrm{ed}}\,\mathrm{roles}_a
 \;-\; w_{\mathrm{sen}}\,\bigl[\log(1+\mathrm{works}_a)-\mathrm{med}\bigr]_+ .
\end{align*}
\emph{Text similarity} ($\lambda$, $k$): max pooling versus top-$k$ mean, the standard design choice (OpenReview defaults to max; the ACL matcher weights the top three cosines $1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13$). \emph{Citation bonus}: authors of papers the manuscript cites, saturating in the count; this is how editors find reviewers by hand and needs no full text on the index side. \emph{Seeds}: $\sigma_a$ is the cosine between $a$'s profile (mean paper embedding) and the closest user-named seed reviewer, centred on the candidate pool because profile cosines cluster near~1. \emph{Volume correction}: the expected best-of-$n$ cosine of $n$ random papers, from the query's own similarity distribution under a normal approximation, removes the advantage prolific authors get from having many draws. \emph{Editorial and seniority penalties} use the side tables (current roles count 1, past roles 0.5). \emph{Early-career bonus} $\phi$ fades linearly in $\log$ works to zero at a cutoff. Optional recency weighting shrinks $s_i$ toward $\mu_s$ by $0.5^{\text{age}/h}$; multiplying raw cosines instead is meaningless because dense-encoder cosines all lie in roughly $[0.7,1]$.

Scoring is exact but restricted to a candidate pool: a vectorised per-author maximum selects the top 2{,}000 (or $100n$) authors plus anyone cited or seeded; penalties only lower scores, so nothing outside the pool could rank above it. Re-ranking 100k authors takes about 40~ms. If a diversity parameter $d$ is set, the pool is re-ordered by maximal marginal relevance, $(1-d)\,\mathrm{score}(a)-d\max_{b\in\text{picked}\cup\text{seeds}}\cos(a,b)$, so consecutive picks cover different neighbourhoods.

\paragraph{Conflicts.} The user names the manuscript's authors; they are resolved to index authors by normalised name (exact, then surname+initials; ambiguities reported). Their co-authors on papers within a window (default 5 years), people at the same institution, genealogy links, and a persistent user-declared list are \emph{flagged} with the reason rather than dropped, because name resolution is fuzzy; an exclude mode removes them.

\section{Evaluation}
Leave-one-out: sample 200 indexed papers; for each, mask it, use its own embedding as the manuscript and its OpenAlex reference list as the bibliography, rank all authors, and record where its true authors land (authors whose only paper is the held-out one are unreachable and excluded). This rewards recovering the \emph{actual} authors among 100k candidates, which favours prolific people; it is a proxy for fit, not a measure of it.

In the only controlled human comparison we know of (Stelmakh et al., \emph{A Gold Standard Dataset for the Reviewer Assignment Problem}), title-plus-abstract SPECTER-family models with max pooling were the best performers and full-text TPMS gained only modestly from full text; ORMatch is in that family. Full-text indexing is further limited here by 18\% strict OA-PDF coverage and the absence of any text-mining route for INFORMS journals.

\section{Limitations}
Abstract coverage of Elsevier venues (37\% missing) until an Elsevier key is used; OpenAlex author disambiguation splits and merges people; PhD students appear only if they have an indexed paper; roster and board scraping is blocked by many sites; all weights are hand-set (no elicited reviewer ratings), so they are exposed as controls with the objective displayed rather than fixed.
